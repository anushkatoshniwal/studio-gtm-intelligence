## Executive synthesis

The customer-call dataset shows recurring value alongside recurring friction. Every segment reports repeat usage or clear purchase intent, but each also attaches conditions to continued adoption. Creators need simpler setup, reliable pronunciation, and a lower-volume package. Agencies need collaborative review, reusable language assets, emotional control, and predictable campaign costs. Enterprises require governance, measurable quality, and predictable annual commitments.

The strongest signals are purchase intent and enterprise requirements (5/5). Quality, pricing, missing capability, and repeat-usage signals are consistently strong (4/5). Activation friction is present but somewhat weaker (3/5), implying that the largest commercial barriers in this demo evidence appear after initial setup: quality control, collaboration, governance, and packaging.

## Creator segment

### Key observed behaviours and patterns

- First-project activation is hindered by uncertainty about source-audio format and manual retries for fast-paced video.
- Output quality is directionally positive for Hindi, but pronunciation of English brand names in Tamil creates inconsistency.
- The desired capability is precise correction: edit pronunciation without regenerating the whole voiceover.
- Weekly return usage is linked to improved watch time for regional-language versions.
- Purchase intent exists, but it is conditional on a lower-volume bundle and creator monetization.

### Repeated signals

- Two separate insights describe first-workflow friction.
- Two quality-related insights converge on pronunciation control.
- Pricing resistance and purchase intent point to the same packaging issue: individual creators need a plan aligned with lower volume and uncertain income.

### Supporting evidence

- Setup uncertainty around source-audio format. `[activation_friction; strength 3/5; synthetic-demo-call:creator; 2026-08-20 20:15:58]`
- Manual retries to align speech with fast-paced video. `[activation_friction; strength 3/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Weekly usage because localized versions reportedly improve watch time. `[repeat_usage; strength 4/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Strong purchase intent for a lower-volume plan with dubbing, captions, and reusable voice settings. `[purchase_intent; strength 5/5; same source/timestamp]`

### Contradictory evidence and tensions

- Positive Hindi quality is offset by inconsistent cross-language pronunciation.
- Weekly value is reported, yet the paid plan is considered difficult to justify before reliable sponsorship income.
- Purchase intent is strong but conditional; it should not be treated as an unconditional willingness to pay.

### Important unknowns

- The price and usage allowance at which creators would actually convert.
- How frequently pronunciation failures occur across languages and content types.
- Whether watch-time improvement is measured or perceived.
- The abandonment rate caused by first-project retries.
- Whether reusable voice settings or captions have greater influence on purchase.

## Media-agency segment

### Key observed behaviours and patterns

- Collaboration is the most repeated capability gap: reviewers cannot comment at precise moments, and agencies want client workspaces, reviewer roles, and project access controls.
- Output quality is acceptable for narration, but advertising requires more emotional control and reusable approved terminology.
- Repeat weekly use across three brands indicates ongoing campaign relevance.
- Usage-based pricing is difficult to forecast because revisions are variable.
- Purchase intent centers on a successful multilingual client pilot and an annual plan.

### Repeated signals

- Two missing-capability signals independently point to collaborative review and permissions.
- Two quality-control signals point to tone and terminology consistency.
- Pricing predictability appears both as an objection and as a condition for annual purchase.

### Supporting evidence

- Activation slowed because reviewers could not comment on precise output moments. `[missing_capability; strength 4/5; synthetic-demo-call:media-agency; 2026-08-20 20:15:58]`
- Requested client workspaces, reviewer roles, and project-level controls. `[missing_capability; strength 4/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Weekly usage across three active consumer brands. `[repeat_usage; strength 4/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Annual-plan intent after a successful multilingual launch for a major client. `[purchase_intent; strength 5/5; same source/timestamp]`

### Contradictory evidence and tensions

- Voice naturalness is rated positively for narration, while emotional control remains insufficient for advertising.
- Repeat usage demonstrates relevance, but unpredictable revision volume creates a pricing objection.
- Annual purchase intent depends on a successful client outcome, making the client—not only the agency—the effective decision constraint.

### Important unknowns

- Required review features and integrations for client approval.
- Typical revision volume and its effect on gross margin.
- The fixed bundle size agencies consider predictable but not restrictive.
- Whether reusable pronunciation libraries materially reduce cycle time.
- Decision authority between agency operations, editors, buyers, and end clients.

## Enterprise segment

### Key observed behaviours and patterns

- Procurement and security review create more activation friction than product setup.
- Governance needs are explicit: single sign-on, audit logs, role-based access, and regional data-handling documentation.
- Quality requirements focus on consistent pronunciation across hundreds of technical terms.
- Buyers prefer predictable annual pricing with committed usage.
- Daily pilot usage supports operational relevance, but purchase remains conditional on security approval, accuracy, and turnaround targets.

### Repeated signals

- Security appears in both early friction and the final purchase condition.
- Accuracy appears in terminology consistency and pilot success criteria.
- Predictability recurs in governance, quality, turnaround, and annual pricing expectations.

### Supporting evidence

- Procurement and security review dominate activation friction. `[enterprise_requirement; strength 5/5; synthetic-demo-call:enterprise; 2026-08-20 20:15:58]`
- Required SSO, audit logs, RBAC, and regional data documentation. `[enterprise_requirement; strength 5/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Daily pilot usage for onboarding and compliance localization. `[repeat_usage; strength 4/5; same source/timestamp]`
- Purchase intent after security approval if accuracy and turnaround targets are met. `[purchase_intent; strength 5/5; same source/timestamp]`

### Contradictory evidence and tensions

- Narration is described as clear, but technical pronunciation consistency remains a requirement.
- Daily pilot activity signals value, but it does not override security and procurement gates.
- Purchase intent is substantial but explicitly conditional on multiple acceptance criteria.

### Important unknowns

- Exact security, data-residency, retention, and audit requirements.
- Accuracy and turnaround thresholds by content type and language.
- Length and ownership of procurement and security review.
- Minimum annual commitment buyers will accept.
- Whether daily pilot usage persists after the initial localization backlog.

## Cross-segment changes and trends

- **Control requirements increase with organizational complexity.** Creators want self-serve pronunciation correction; agencies want review workflows; enterprises want governance and auditability.
- **Pricing predictability is a shared concern with different forms.** Creators want a lower-volume package, agencies want revision-safe bundles, and enterprises want committed annual pricing.
- **Purchase intent is consistently conditional.** Each segment names a proof point: creator plan fit, agency client success, or enterprise security and performance acceptance.
- **Quality moves beyond naturalness.** The repeated need is controllability—pronunciation, emotion, reusable terminology, and measurable accuracy.
